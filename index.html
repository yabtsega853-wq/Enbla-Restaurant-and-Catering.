Since 2018 · Bole, Addis Ababa

Three generations of ceremony, one small room in Bole.

Tukiya began as a family recipe — a jebena passed down from grandmother to mother to daughter. Today we roast, grind, and pour the same way: slowly, out loud, and always with room for one more cup.

Every pot is poured three times. Every guest is welcomed the same. That's the ritual.

Read the ritual →
01

Abol

The first round. Raw green beans are washed and roasted over hot coals, the smoke drifting through the room as an invitation for everyone to gather.

02

Tona

The second round. Brewed slowly in the clay Jebena, this pour is considered the richest — deep, dark, and full of body.

03

Baraka

The blessing. The final round, shared in community, respect, and togetherness — a quiet thank-you before the cups are set down.

01

Hand-picked

Yirgacheffe and Sidamo beans, sourced from small farms we visit twice a year. Nothing older than seven days in the bag.

02

Ceremony, daily

Three rounds — Abol, Tona, Baraka — poured at 4pm sharp, every day except Sunday. Pull up a chair, stay a while.

03

Roasted here

Every bag is roasted in-house in small batches, right here in Bole. You can smell it from the street.
